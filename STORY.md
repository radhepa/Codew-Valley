# Story bible

## The premise

Three hundred years ago the valley held **Willowmere**, a village built by
Ysolde the Rune-Weaver. Its people spoke the Old Tongue (Python) to the rune
stones, and the stones built, lit and fed the village. Then came **the Long
Quiet**: the stones stopped answering, the people left, and the forest took
the valley back.

Only Pip stayed: a small serpent who talks too much, hums when nervous, and
keeps the last rune stone warm. You arrive with a deed to "an empty valley"
and become the first person in three centuries to speak the Old Tongue there.

Three threads run through the whole game:

1. **The mystery of Old Willowmere.** Ruins turn up as you build: a carved
   millstone in the field, a bell in the pond, a sealed vault under the
   library. Each building gives a **Chronicle page** in Ysolde's own words,
   and together they explain the Long Quiet.
2. **The Charter.** At the end of Act I, Lord Aldous Vane of the Vane
   Trading Company arrives. No royal charter means the land is the Company's,
   and he'd like to drain the pond and quarry the hills. He's pompous rather
   than evil, and the village beats him with the one thing he respects:
   paperwork. In Python.
3. **Pip's secret.** Pip never leaves the rune stone, flinches at the north
   hill, glows when runes are spoken and grows faint when the Hush comes. At
   the Observatory we learn why. The finale sets Pip free.

The theme, said once and gently near the midpoint: **a language lives only
while it is taught.** The Long Quiet came because a few experts kept the Old
Tongue to themselves. By the end the whole village speaks it, because you
learned it and passed it on.

**Epilogue:** a festival with every villager, Pip's first walk through the
town, and the Rune Workshop opens: code that runs the village.

## Why it feels rewarding

- **Every challenge:** a piece of the building pops into place with a camera
  move, plus a Pip or villager line that moves the story along.
- **Mid-building:** a short villager scene, usually where a ruin or keepsake
  turns up.
- **Every building:** a new villager moves in, a Chronicle page, a
  celebration where everyone gathers, a keepsake for the Founder's Journal,
  and a change you can see in the valley (the bell rings, the sails turn,
  stalls fill, the clock ticks).
- **Ranks:** Camp → **Hamlet** (2 buildings) → **Village** (4) → **Town**
  (charter, 9) → **Rune Town** (12), with the sign at the valley entrance
  changing each time.
- **Act endings are cliffhangers:** Vane's writ (Act I), the Hush and Pip's
  fading (Act II).
- **Foreshadowing to reread:** Pip's odd lines, the north hill, glowing
  scales. Every hint pays off in Act III.

## Writing rules

- Cozy first: gentle humour, no deaths, no real villains. Vane is redeemable.
- Pip is warm, a little dramatic, and gives hints, never answers.
- Every villager gets one personal want, which is where Phase 2's heart
  events start: Bram's lost caravan friends, Wren's family mill, Marlo's
  grandmother, Sable's rival, Hazel and Bram, Ingrid's old crew, Odo's
  unfinished book, Mirela's garden, Thistle's re-election, Tock's
  apprentice, Rook's last adventure, Vane's grandfather.
- Story lines live in the content (`opening`, `intro`, `done`, `finale`,
  `chatDuring`, `chatAfter`); "Name: text" lines are spoken by that villager.
